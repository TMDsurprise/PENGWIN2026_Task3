- **DDP:** Distributed Data Parallel, the PyTorch multi-GPU data-parallel training method.
- **GPU / CPU:** Graphics processing unit / central processing unit.
- **LR:** Learning rate.
- **RA:** Rotation-Aware, the rotation-aware training stage in our method.
- **OHM:** Online hard-example mining.
- **OOF:** Out-of-fold prediction. Each patient's calibration prediction is generated by a model that

- **SFQ:** Small-Fragment Query, the small-fragment query branch.
- **SFQ-F:** The fragment self-attention version of SFQ, using equally weighted fragment tokens.
- **SFQ-FX:** SFQ-F augmented with cross-attention, allowing a fragment to query the complete-pelvis

- **B1 / B2 / B3:** The identifiers of three complementary candidate branches in our e26 stage; they

- **E2, e3, e25, e26, and e27:** Internal experiment and checkpoint identifiers. They do not denote
  epoch numbers or standard models in the field.
- **C2:** Our internal abbreviation for the stage-2 conservative candidate-comparison gate. It uses
  predicted benefit, severe-failure risk, and fallback conditions to decide whether to accept a
  non-safety candidate.
- **TRE:** Target registration error, reported in millimeters.
- **Trans:** Translation error, reported in millimeters.
- **Rot:** Rotation error, reported in degrees.

- **4 x 4 matrix:** A homogeneous rigid-transformation matrix containing a 3 x 3 rotation, a 3 x 1

## 7. Method description

We formulate Task 3 as a paired-coordinate-regression problem for multi-fragment rigid assembly. The
input consists of the surface meshes of the sacral (SA), left innominate (LI), and right innominate
(RI) fracture fragments from one patient. The output is a `4 x 4` rigid reduction matrix for every
fragment. The complete algorithm has two parts: a coordinate backbone that predicts reduced
coordinates point by point, followed by candidate generation, candidate ranking, and conservative
risk control.

### 7.1 Part I: coordinate backbone

The coordinate backbone is based on the Assembly Transformer from the PENGWIN 2026 Task 3 Baseline.
It jointly processes all fragment points in a case, their surface normals, fragment IDs, and bone
types. Global point-level attention predicts a paired target coordinate in the reduced pelvis for
every input point. For each fragment, rotation and translation are recovered from the source points
and predicted target points using an SVD-based Kabsch/Horn solver. This process is iterated for at most
10 steps and terminates early when the coordinate update falls below 2 mm.

The final e25 backbone has the following sequential training lineage:

1. **Clean Baseline:** trained from scratch on challenge simulation data through `epoch=659`, i.e.,
   660 epochs.
2. **Rotation-aware continuation:** initialized from Clean e659 and continued for 20 epochs, with
   `epoch=19` selected. In addition to point-wise coordinate MSE, we introduced fragment-balanced
   coordinates, centered vectors, long-baseline point pairs, cross-covariance, and differentiable
   Horn/Kabsch rotation and translation supervision, allowing rotation gradients to act directly on
   the coordinate prediction head.
3. **Rotation anti-forgetting OHM:** initialized from rotation-aware e19 and continued for 25 epochs,
   with `epoch=24` selected. The training process maintains hard-example pools according to bone type,
   fragment size, and historical failure mode. Replay and rotation-teacher consistency reduce the
   catastrophic forgetting caused by learning only from hard examples.

The final e25 coordinate backbone therefore inherits `660 + 20 + 25 = 705` sequential training
epochs. All three stages were trained with two RTX 5090 GPUs using DDP and mixed precision. Because
the stages use different sampling policies and losses, 705 epochs describe the actual weight lineage,
not 705 repetitions of an identical optimization procedure.

At inference time, Area64 sampling is applied independently to each bone. We sample 5,000 surface
points from each of SA, LI, and RI, allocate the within-bone budget according to triangle surface area,
and guarantee at least 64 points for every fragment. Area64 is a deterministic preprocessing policy
and introduces no additional training epochs.

### 7.2 Part II: candidate generation, ranking, and safety decisions

#### 7.2.1 Original four candidates and the e3 Ranker

The four candidate slots of the original e3 Ranker are defined during training by
`alpha=[0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.25]`. In the frozen submission, Clinical170 OOF calibration fixed the effective
source alphas of the exact-full online path to `[0, 1.0, 1.25, 1.25]`. The later outer e27 stage uses
`[0, 1.0, 1.0, 1.0]` as the candidate-source encoding for its four heterogeneous complete-pelvis
candidates.

The original Ranker was not trained from scratch. Its actual lineage used the historical E2 Ranker at
`epoch=119` (120 epochs) for initialization. We then froze the e25 coordinate backbone and performed
30 epochs of e25-specific strict Ranker recalibration on two RTX 5090 GPUs, obtaining `epoch=29`.
Finally, the Ranker was continued at a lower learning rate, with continuation `epoch=3` selected, i.e.,
four additional epochs. The final original e3 Ranker therefore has a 154-epoch optimization lineage,
of which the final 34 epochs specifically adapt it to the e25 backbone.

When importing historical e119, we load only the Ranker parameters and explicitly exclude the old
checkpoint's coordinate-backbone parameters so that the historical OHM backbone cannot overwrite
e25. The original Ranker and its internal C2 gate jointly produce a stable baseline-selected safety
pose.

#### 7.2.2 SFQ-F, SFQ-FX, and exact-full

SFQ-F constructs one equally weighted token for each fragment and applies fragment-level
self-attention. This prevents small fragments from being overwhelmed by large fragments in a global
point-level representation simply because they contain fewer sampled points. SFQ-FX additionally
uses cross-attention from fragment queries to a complete-pelvis patch memory. Both branches apply
conservative corrections to the e25 pose through a zero-initialized, gated `SE(3)` residual head.

Each of the SFQ-F and SFQ-FX branches was first trained for a three-epoch screening/preheating stage
and then continued for nine epochs with seed 42 and a low learning rate. Each final branch therefore
underwent 12 epochs of formal optimization. On a four-RTX-5090 server, the experiments were run in
parallel with one GPU allocated to each experiment; an individual branch did not use two-GPU DDP.
A 32-case, 100-epoch capacity-overfit experiment was used only to confirm that the architecture was
learnable and did not contribute weights to the submission.

The original four candidates and SFQ are not alternatives. The output of the original e3 Ranker and
C2 is always retained as the safety fallback, whereas SFQ-F and SFQ-FX only provide additional
fragment-aware correction candidates. A low-capacity Huber-ridge calibrator is first fitted with
patient-level five-fold OOF predictions and then refitted on the full calibration set. Together with
an internal C2 gate using `margin=0.15`, it decides whether to accept an SFQ correction and forms the
exact-full safety candidate. Huber-ridge calibration and C2 are not neural-network training epochs.

#### 7.2.3 Complementary e26 candidates

In addition to exact-full, the algorithm retains three structurally or distributionally
complementary complete-pelvis candidates:

- **B1:** adds an equal-fragment context adapter to the later layers of e25. It was trained for three
  complete epochs, with `epoch=1` selected.
- **B2:** adds a point-reliability auxiliary output and a robust fragment-coordinate loss on top of
  B1. It was trained for three complete epochs, with `epoch=2` selected. The final rigid solver still
  uses uniformly weighted Kabsch/Horn.
- **B3:** adds Qsmall hard sampling, replay, and OHM on top of B2. It was trained for eight complete
  epochs, with `epoch=6` selected.

B1, B2, and B3 were trained in parallel on a four-RTX-5090 server, with one GPU assigned to each
branch rather than two GPUs per branch.

#### 7.2.4 e27 complete-pelvis Ranker and outer C2

The final outer candidate pool is candidate 0 = exact-full, candidate 1 = B1, candidate 2 = B2, and
candidate 3 = B3. Each candidate completes an entire iterative pelvis reduction and is mapped into
the same canonical state. The e27 Ranker jointly evaluates four complete-pelvis assemblies instead of
evaluating an isolated point or fragment. It uses backbone fragment features, candidate geometry,
bone/candidate/fragment embeddings, and a three-layer context Transformer to predict candidate
scores, TRE/Trans/Rot/CD quality proxies, and severe-failure probabilities.

The e27 Ranker was trained on 8,617 simulation training samples and 958 patient-held-out validation
samples. Each sample cached up to 10 rollout iterations from all four candidate sources. The maximum
training length was 30 epochs, validation was performed every epoch, and early-stopping patience was
eight. The frozen model is `LR=2e-5, seed=42, epoch=11`, i.e., the weights after the 12th epoch. The
30-epoch maximum is only an upper limit and must not be reported as the number of epochs actually
used.

The outer C2 gate permits a replacement of candidate 0 only when the predicted aggregate error of a
new candidate improves by at least 0.05, its severe risk does not increase, and its severe-risk
probability does not exceed 0.2. A patient-level rollback restores the safety candidate when overall
case risk increases. C2 is a fixed decision rule and has no training epochs.

## 8. Main technical contributions and/or novel components

1. **Area64 small-fragment-preserving sampling:** allocates points by surface area while guaranteeing
   at least 64 points per fragment.
2. **Paired-coordinate-driven rotation-aware training:** applies centered-vector, long-baseline-pair,
   cross-covariance, and differentiable Horn/Kabsch supervision directly to the coordinate head.
3. **Anti-forgetting OHM:** stratifies hard examples by bone, size, and failure mode and protects
   ordinary-case performance through replay and teacher consistency.
4. **Small-Fragment Query:** provides small fragments with independent global query and correction
   capacity through equal-weight fragment tokens, complete-pelvis cross-attention, and a
   zero-initialized `SE(3)` residual.
5. **Heterogeneous complete-pelvis candidate pool:** retains exact-full, fragment-context,
   point-reliability, and hard-replay complete assemblies rather than collecting only similar
   checkpoints.
6. **Complete-pelvis combination Ranker:** jointly evaluates SA/LI/RI geometric consistency, metric
   proxies, and severe-failure risk.
7. **Two-level conservative risk gating:** both the internal exact-full gate and the final outer
   C2/patient rollback preserve explicit safety fallbacks.

## 9. Complete pipeline

1. Parse SA, LI, RI, fragment IDs, surface points, and normals from the input OBJ meshes.
2. Sample 5,000 points per bone, allocate points by surface area, and retain at least 64 points per
   fragment.
3. Center the complete case and apply isotropic scale normalization.
4. Use the e25 coordinate backbone to predict paired target coordinates and recover each fragment's
   rigid update with Kabsch/Horn.
5. Run four-candidate iteration with the frozen deployment source alphas `[0, 1.0, 1.25, 1.25]`, and
   obtain a safety baseline pose using the e3 Ranker and C2.
6. Generate small-fragment correction candidates with SFQ-F and SFQ-FX, and obtain exact-full
   candidate 0 through internal calibration and C2.
7. Run e26 B1, B2, and B3 independently to obtain candidates 1-3.
8. Map the four complete-pelvis pose sets into the same canonical state.
9. Use the e27 Ranker to predict candidate ranking, metric proxies, and severe-failure risk.
10. Use outer C2 and patient-level rollback to decide whether candidate 0 should be replaced.
11. Normalize the accumulated transformations using SA fragment 1 as the rigid anchor.
12. Output a `4 x 4` reduction-pose matrix in JSON format for every fragment.

## 10. Use of external data

No external dataset was used. The neural candidate generators and e27 Ranker were trained using the
simulation data supplied by PENGWIN 2026. The challenge-provided Clinical170 training cases were used
for method ablation, patient-level five-fold OOF prediction, low-capacity Huber-ridge/C2 calibration,
and frozen-pipeline replay; they are therefore not external data. Clinical labels were not used for
gradient-based updates of the e25 coordinate backbone, the SFQ candidate generators, or the e27
neural Ranker. The hidden test set and its labels remained inaccessible throughout development.

## 11. Use of externally pretrained models

We did not use PointMAE, TotalSegmentator, or any other externally pretrained model or external
weights. All initialization checkpoints were produced by our team using challenge-provided simulation
data.

## 12. Preprocessing techniques

- Parse bone type and fragment ID from OBJ meshes and uniformly sample surface points and normals from
  the triangular meshes.
- Sample exactly 5,000 points for each of SA, LI, and RI; allocate the within-bone budget by triangle
  surface area and retain at least 64 points per fragment.
- Center the complete case and apply isotropic scale normalization.
- Fix the NumPy and PyTorch seed to 42 per case for reproducible sampling and inference.
- Construct metadata including fragment count, diameter, centroid, covariance, and bone type.
- Normalize the final output using SA fragment 1 as the rigid anchor.

## 13. Data augmentation techniques

- Random fragment combinations and random `SE(3)` rotations and translations generated online from
  the challenge simulation data.
- Random anchors, fragment merging, fragment counts, and fracture patterns.
- Point dropping, region dropout, surface resampling, and coordinate jitter.
- Stratified sampling by SA/LI/RI, fragment size, and historical failure mode.
- Qsmall hard sampling and Clean/e25 replay.
- Conditional sampling across different fragment sizes and rigid-offset ranges.

### 14.2 Validation and model selection

- Clean/RA/OHM used patient-held-out simulation validation with early stopping or milestone-based
  checkpoint selection.
- The e25 Ranker froze the coordinate backbone and selected checkpoints using simulation
  `val/oracle_regret`.
- SFQ used a fixed simulation validation set, zero controls, and Clinical170 candidate-oracle
  diagnostics. Capacity overfitting was used only to establish learnability, not as evidence of
  generalization.
- Clinical170 calibration used patient-level five-fold OOF predictions. Patients held out in each
  fold did not participate in fitting, early stopping, or gate search for their own predictions.
- e27 used an 8,617/958 patient-level simulation split, validation every epoch, patience eight, and
  froze seed42 e11.
- Hidden-test labels did not participate in training, selection, or inference.

## 15. Loss function(s)

### 15.1 Coordinate backbone

The base coordinate objective is a convex combination rather than a full point-wise MSE plus an
additional 0.25-weighted fragment loss:

```text
L_coord = 0.75 * L_global_point_MSE
        + 0.25 * L_fragment_balanced_MSE

L_RA = L_coord + s(epoch) * (
          0.010  * L_centered_vector
        + 0.004  * L_long_baseline_pair
        + 0.005  * L_cross_covariance
        + 0.010  * L_Horn_rotation
        + 0.0015 * L_Horn_translation
       )
```

Here, `s(epoch)` is the warm-up factor for the rotation auxiliaries. The OHM stage mixes `L_coord`
with case-difficulty-weighted `L_hard`, whose coefficient reaches at most `0.30` after warm-up. The
rotation auxiliaries remain active, and selective rotation-teacher consistency is applied every four
steps with weight `0.020`, only to reliable natural samples.

### 15.2 SFQ residual

```text
L_SFQ = 2.0  * L_rotation_chordal
      + 0.5  * L_translation_SmoothL1
      + 0.5  * L_paired_coordinate_SmoothL1
      + 0.2  * L_preserve
      + 0.02 * L_residual_regularization
```

The first three terms use a fragment-weighted mean with
`w_i = 1 + 2 * qsmall_strength_i`. Translation and paired-coordinate errors are first converted to
millimeters and then normalized by 30 mm. The preserve loss penalizes degradation in Rot, Trans, or
TRE relative to the e25 base pose. The generic SFQ implementation also supports correction-confidence
BCE and point-confidence KL losses. However, the frozen submission uses only the F/FX variants and
does not enable the G/H confidence heads; these two losses are therefore exactly zero in the uploaded
weights.

### 15.3 Candidate Ranker

Candidate utility normalizes TRE, Trans, Rot, and CD using leaderboard reference scales. The Ranker
objective is:

```text
L_rank = 1.0  * L_hard_oracle_CE
       + 0.5  * L_pairwise_ranking
       + 0.25 * L_metric_regression
       + 0.2  * L_severe_BCE
```

The hard oracle is the candidate with the lowest normalized utility. The pairwise loss supervises
only candidate pairs whose utility difference is greater than `0.05`. The metric head uses Smooth-L1
to regress `log(1 + metric)` and applies `expm1` at inference time. CD is a geometry proxy computed
from 64 sampled points per candidate. PA is not included in the final utility. A severe label is
defined as `Rot > 30 degrees` or `Trans > 20 mm`. Huber-ridge calibration and C2 are low-capacity or
deterministic postprocessing components and do not generate the neural losses above.

## 16. Base network architecture

- Assembly Transformer coordinate backbone: dimension 384, 12 layers, and 8 attention heads.
- Output: point-wise paired reduction coordinates; rigid solver: per-fragment SVD Kabsch/Horn.
- SFQ-F/FX: 384-dimensional fragment tokens, a two-layer eight-head fragment Transformer, optional
  pelvis-patch cross-attention, and a continuous 6D rotation plus 3D translation residual head.
- e26 B1: equal-fragment context adapter.
- e26 B2: B1 plus a point-reliability auxiliary head.
- e26 B3: the B2 architecture trained with Qsmall hard sampling, replay, and OHM.
- e27 Ranker: a 27-dimensional candidate-geometry descriptor; bone, candidate, and fragment
  embeddings; a three-layer, hidden-dimension-256, eight-head context Transformer; and score, metric,
  and severe-risk heads.

## 17. Ensembling strategies used during inference

We do not average checkpoint weights or pose matrices. The final system is a candidate-selection
ensemble. Exact-full, B1, B2, and B3 independently generate four complete-pelvis pose sets, which the
e27 Ranker compares jointly. Candidate 0 is always retained as an explicit fallback. A nonzero
candidate is accepted only when it passes the metric margin, severe-risk threshold, and patient-level
rollback conditions. The internal exact-full C2 uses `margin=0.15`. The outer C2 uses
`margin=0.05`, `severe tolerance=0`, and `severe threshold=0.2`.
